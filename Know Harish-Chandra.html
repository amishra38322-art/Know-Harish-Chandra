CHRONOLOGY / 01–05
CHAPTER 01 · 1941–43

A book opens a door

At the University of Allahabad, Harish-Chandra studied theoretical physics after encountering P. A. M. Dirac’s book on quantum mechanics. That early fascination led him into research with Homi J. Bhabha at the Indian Institute of Science in Bangalore.

People around the work

Dirac’s answer about proofs

As recounted in Robert P. Langlands’s biographical memoir, Harish-Chandra worried that his early work with unbounded operators lacked sufficient rigor. Dirac is remembered as replying, “I am not interested in proofs but only in what nature does.” The exchange helped clarify Harish-Chandra’s growing preference for mathematics.

Read the biographical account ↗
A correction that became a friendship

While attending a lecture by Wolfgang Pauli in Cambridge, Harish-Chandra pointed out a mistake in Pauli’s work. The biographical record notes that the two went on to become lifelong friends—a telling reminder that intellectual precision and collegial respect can reinforce one another.

Read the biographical account ↗
Walking conversations with André Weil

During a 1957–58 Guggenheim fellowship in Paris, Harish-Chandra worked with André Weil; the two often went walking together. It is a small but memorable detail from a life often summarized through technical achievements alone.

Read the biographical account ↗
Robert Langlands on the reach of his work

In his biographical memoir, Robert Langlands describes Harish-Chandra as one of the mathematicians who helped transform infinite-dimensional group representation theory from a peripheral topic into a major field of modern mathematics.

Read Langlands’s memoir ↗
Armand Borel and the mathematical legacy

Armand Borel wrote recollections of Harish-Chandra and discussed his contributions to representation theory and harmonic analysis. The larger story is one of work whose techniques connected algebra, analysis, and geometry.
